# InnovateX 2026–27 — Verified Competition Requirements

**Status:** Research checkpoint, not a substitute for the registered-team portal.

**Primary source:** `source/InnovateX_Techfest_2026-27_Brief.pdf` (9 pages, read locally on 2026-10-21; page references below are to that file).

**Current date used for planning:** 2026-10-21 in the team’s stated Asia/Calcutta time zone.

## 1. Official challenge statement

For **Theme 1 — Underground Water Management**, the brief asks:

> How can IoT technologies be designed and deployed to effectively monitor, protect, and manage underground water resources in an urban city?

The brief asks for an innovative IoT-based solution addressing a specific groundwater-related challenge, with examples including water-level monitoring, contamination detection, illegal-extraction prevention, pipeline-leak detection and aquifer-recharge analysis. **PDF pages 1–2.**

The brief describes the domain as connected sensors, monitoring systems and data analytics for groundwater depletion, contamination, illegal extraction, pipeline leakages and unpredictable recharge. **PDF page 1.**

## 2. Official Round 1 requirements

Round 1 is a physical concept pitch at the relevant regional zonal centre. The PDF lists these required content areas:

1. Team information: team name, team ID, theme number and member contact details.
2. A precise problem statement.
3. Background and context, including existing solutions and why they fall short.
4. Proposed solution, technology and operating explanation.
5. Impact and scalability.

Each team receives **10 minutes** to present. The brief says the top five people/teams at each regional zonal centre qualify for Round 2; the wording is reproduced as written and should be clarified with the organisers if needed. **PDF page 5.**

The PDF explicitly says that Round 1 has no separate online submission and that evaluation occurs at the zonal venue. **PDF page 6.**

**Important:** The PDF does **not** mandate five slides. A five-slide web deck is our communication choice, not an official rule.

## 3. Official Round 2 requirements

Teams that qualify for Round 2 must build a prototype and submit a final report. The PDF lists:

- detailed solution description, design and technology;
- prototype demonstration with evidence of functionality and usability;
- cost and market viability;
- implementation plan and adaptation to different conditions;
- scalability across India;
- impact assessment with clear estimates of social and economic benefits.

The supplied PDF lists the final report deadline as **1 December 2026** and final presentations at Techfest, IIT Bombay, on **15–18 December 2026**. **PDF page 6.**

The PDF’s timeline also says registration closes on the date of the relevant city zonal, Round 1 presentation/results are on that city-zonal date, and finals are 15–18 December. **PDF page 7.**

## 4. Official evaluation criteria

The PDF lists the following criteria on pages 7–8:

- creativity and novelty;
- functionality and usability;
- cost and market value;
- technical implementation and feasibility;
- regional scalability;
- environmental and socioeconomic impact;
- design and ergonomics;
- a problem-centric bonus for deep, region-specific understanding.

The PDF specifically calls out operational pressures such as subterranean connectivity, sensor calibration, maintenance, power consumption, durability, data reliability and severe environmental conditions. These are not optional talking points; they should appear in the architecture and risk discussion.

## 5. Originality and compliance constraints

The PDF says solutions must be original and not protected by patents, copyrights or publications. It also says organisers may verify participant details and that organiser/judge decisions are final. **PDF page 8.**

We interpret originality as requiring a truthful, evidence-backed distinction from existing products and programmes—not merely a new dashboard design. A search result is not proof of non-existence, so the pitch must state the limits of the competitor scan.

## 8. Requirements that remain uncertain

- The supplied brief does not state a required slide count, judging weights, hardware bill, prototype form factor or selection probability.
- The supplied PDF uses “top five people” in one sentence; it does not make the team-versus-person qualification unit unambiguous.
- The city-zonal venue, registration state and any later amendments must be checked by the Team Leader in the registered portal.
- The current web page is a live source and may supersede the PDF for schedule updates. Preserve a screenshot or portal confirmation before the pitch.
